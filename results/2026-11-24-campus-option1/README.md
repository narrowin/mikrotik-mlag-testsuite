Resets the data bridge on all three CHRs over management SSH: protocol-mode=none and forward-reserved-addresses=no on both MLAG peers, protocol-mode=rstp priority=0x0000 on the access switch, with edge=yes on its uplink bond and edge=yes, bpdu-guard=yes and loop-protect=on on its host port ether4, and reads the access switch port settings back. It requires a loss-free probe baseline, no BPDU from or through the MLAG pair and one copy of each broadcast. It then emulates a power loss of CHR 1 (MLAG primary): in one step it takes the container veths behind CHR 1's ether2 to ether4 down, sets the facing NICs of CHR 2 and the access switch link-down over QMP and pauses CHR 1's guest with QMP stop, then confirms carrier loss at every neighbour. After 20 s it powers CHR 1 on with QMP system_reset and cont, brings the links back up, waits for the guest uptime to reset and keeps probing for 16 s plus a 3 s recovery window. Run it with `mlag-lab run --case medium-core-off-power-loss-primary`.

- **Primary off:** Every link to CHR 1 loses carrier at once, as on a real power loss. CHR 2 sees its peer port ether2 go down and reverts its MLAG bonds to its own LACP system ID, so the access switch and traffic host 2 renegotiate LACP, with brief loss. They drop their member to CHR 1 on carrier loss, without waiting for the LACP short timeout. The access switch's uplink is an edge port, so it forwards as soon as LACP has reselected. [Source](https://manual.mikrotik.com/docs/high-availability-solutions/multi-chassis-link-aggregation-group/)
- **Primary back:** Carrier returns while CHR 1 boots. CHR 1 keeps its bond members out of the bond until the MLAG system ID syncs (mlag-init-delay), rejoins as MLAG primary and restores the shared system ID. The access switch and traffic host 2 renegotiate LACP to the shared system ID with brief loss, and the edge uplink forwards again at once. [Source](https://manual.mikrotik.com/docs/high-availability-solutions/multi-chassis-link-aggregation-group/)
