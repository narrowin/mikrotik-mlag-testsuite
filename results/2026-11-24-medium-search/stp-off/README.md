### Baseline · all bridges STP off · STP off

Reset the data bridge on all three CHRs over management SSH with protocol-mode=none (priority 0x1000 on both MLAG peers, 0x8000 on the access switch). Then run numbered 100 ms pings for 4 s in both VLAN 110 directions and both bypass directions, read back bridge, MLAG and LACP state, and send three ARP broadcasts each way. No fault is applied. Run it with `mlag-lab run --case medium-stp-off-baseline`.

| Phase | Expected | Limit | Run 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | Both MLAG peers show mlag-state connected once ICCP is established, with CHR 1 as MLAG primary (lower mlag-priority) and CHR 2 as MLAG secondary. | no loss | ✅ PASS · no loss |

- **Baseline:** Both MLAG peers show mlag-state connected once ICCP is established, with CHR 1 as MLAG primary (lower mlag-priority) and CHR 2 as MLAG secondary. Every bond member of the MLAG bonds and of the access switch uplink is synchronized, collecting and distributing. With protocol-mode=none no bridge sends or forwards BPDUs, each ARP broadcast arrives exactly once, and all four streams deliver with zero loss. [Source](https://manual.mikrotik.com/docs/high-availability-solutions/multi-chassis-link-aggregation-group/#mlag-settings-and-monitoring)

### MLAG secondary reboot · Medium lab, all bridges STP off · STP off

Resets all three bridges over management SSH with protocol-mode=none. It requires a loss-free probe baseline, zero BPDUs and one copy of each broadcast. It then reboots CHR 2 (MLAG secondary, mlag-priority=20) with `/system reboot`, waits for the guest uptime to reset and keeps probing for 16 s plus a 3 s recovery window. Run it with `mlag-lab run --case medium-stp-off-reboot-secondary`.

| Phase | Expected | Limit | Run 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Secondary down | CHR 1 (MLAG primary) keeps the shared system ID, so the access switch's uplink bond and traffic host 2 keep their LACP partner and forward over their members to CHR 1. | ≤ 5 s | ✅ PASS · 2.40 to 2.60 s |
| Secondary back | CHR 2 keeps its bond members out of the 802.3ad bond until the MLAG system ID syncs with CHR 1 (mlag-init-delay), then adds them under the unchanged shared system ID. | ≤ 5 s | ✅ PASS · 0.11 to 0.32 s |

- **Secondary down:** CHR 1 (MLAG primary) keeps the shared system ID, so the access switch's uplink bond and traffic host 2 keep their LACP partner and forward over their members to CHR 1. The virtual links keep carrier, so each partner drops its member to CHR 2 only at the LACP short timeout (3 × 1 s). With protocol-mode=none no port changes state and no BPDUs appear. [Source](https://manual.mikrotik.com/docs/high-availability-solutions/multi-chassis-link-aggregation-group/)
- **Secondary back:** CHR 2 keeps its bond members out of the 802.3ad bond until the MLAG system ID syncs with CHR 1 (mlag-init-delay), then adds them under the unchanged shared system ID. The access switch and traffic host 2 re-add the member to the same aggregator, so loss is brief or none. [Source](https://manual.mikrotik.com/docs/high-availability-solutions/multi-chassis-link-aggregation-group/)
